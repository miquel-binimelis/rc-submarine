# 2026-11-22 - First CAD model: hull tube

**Goal for today:** model the PVC hull tube in Fusion and check its volume.

**What I did:**
- Set up the repository and uploaded the specification.
- Modelled the hull tube: 110 mm outer diameter, 3.2 mm wall, 900 mm long.
- Checked the dimensions with the Measure tool.

**Result:** material volume of the tube = ___ cm3 (expected ~966 cm3).

**What it means:**
- The tube alone weighs ~1.35 kg (PVC ~1.4 g/cm3).
- Sealed with end caps, it displaces ~8.55 L, i.e. ~8.55 kg of buoyancy.
- So there is ~7 kg of capacity for batteries, electronics and ballast.

**Open question:** does all the internal equipment fit in a 103.6 mm bore?
To be checked in a packaging study before buying anything.

**Next step:** measure the pool and ask about the 3D printers.
## Update: end cap

- Modelled an end cap v0: 103 mm plug, 40 mm long, two O-ring grooves
  (4.5 mm wide, 2.2 mm deep) and an 8 mm flange (120 mm diameter).
- The flange stops the cap from being pushed into the tube by external
  water pressure (~25 N at 3 m).
- Tube volume check: 966.3 cm3, matching the hand calculation.

**Open points:** real PVC pipe has a loose inner-diameter tolerance, and
FDM-printed sealing surfaces can leak. The design will be validated with a
vacuum test before anything else is built.
## Update: hull assembly (v0)

- Chose hull shape A: teardrop (Albacore-style) body of revolution, about
  7:1 length-to-diameter ratio, with a printed free-flooding fairing around
  a sealed tube.
- Shortened the pressure tube from 900 mm to 600 mm: with ~200 mm bow and
  stern fairings the total length stays about 1 m.
- Assembled the tube with two end caps (the second is a mirrored copy of
  the first). Interference check: none.
- Free internal length between the caps: 520 mm (about 4.4 L).

**Mistake and fix:** the offset plane used for the mirror was created at
